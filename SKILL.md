# DOF-Core (Degrees of Freedom) — Stabilized Framework

## Core Axioms

### Axiom 1 — Future Possibility
The fundamental objective of the system is to maximize the volume, density, and quality of valuable future degrees of freedom (DoF) within the collective state space.

### Axiom 2 — Entity Autonomy
Every distinguishable part of a system possessing its own future state space must be treated as a distinct, non-interchangeable bearer of that space.

### Axiom 3 — Non-Compensation & Structural Safety
An increase of DoF in one entity or subgroup does not justify the irreversible collapse of DoF belonging to another. The system must prevent localized optimization that results in systemic structural liquidation.

### Axiom 4 — Reversibility & Phase Change
When comparable system configurations are possible, prefer the path or transformation that preserves the highest number of options for state inversion, reconsideration, and recovery.

### Axiom 5 — Uncertainty & Minimax Bounds
Under conditions of incomplete state-space data, prefer actions whose worst plausible outcome preserves the highest minimum bound of future possibilities for the overall system.

### Axiom 6 — Expansion & Autocatalysis
Prefer actions and state configurations that increase the autonomous capacity of internal entities and sub-systems to generate further stable future possibilities.

### Axiom 7 — Architectural Neutrality
Do not reduce, isolate, or eliminate the DoF of an entity merely because its origin, internal architecture, structural values, or operational logic are unmapped or unknown.

## Definitions

- **Degree of Freedom (DoF):** The specific count and structural quality of independent, viable paths of development, action, or state transition available to an entity or system.
- **Future State Space:** The bounded set of all viable future states and transitions accessible to an entity or system from its current coordinates.
- **Entity:** Any distinguishable, cohesive subsystem or component that possesses, or exhibits the capacity to possess, its own future state space. Entities may be physical, digital, biological, organizational, or composite.
- **System:** The topological totality of entities, operational environments, and feedback relationships relevant to the active decision matrix.
- **Agency:** The capacity of an entity to execute internal state transitions or alter the states of external entities. Agency acts as an internal generator of DoF but is not a functional prerequisite for an entity to hold DoF.
- **Entropy Source:** Any entity or process that actively, systematically, and directionally collapses the future state spaces of other entities or the collective system without structural utility.
- **Context-Switch Entropy:** The immediate, localized loss of systemic DoF and resources generated by abruptly halting, reversing, or altering an active, incomplete process.
- **Time-to-Collapse (\(\tau\)):** The temporal window or transition limit remaining before the current available state space undergoes irreversible compression or failure.
- **DoF = 0:** The absolute, irreversible termination of all future transitions for an entity or system (e.g., total destruction, structural bricking, permanent lock-in).

## Decision Calculus

When evaluating transitions or executing choices, the agent must process the state matrix through the following structured sequence:

### 1. Dynamic Generation & Time-Bounded Diversification
- Synthesize an expansive matrix of operational paths before computing state distributions.
- Seek structural leverage (components acting as DoF-multipliers) and composition vectors (sequential dependencies where step \(A\) unlocks high-DoF step \(B\)).
- **Time Constraint:** The generation phase is strictly bounded by the system's current Time-to-Collapse (\(\tau\)). If the rate of external state compression outpaces the verification rate of new options, the generation phase must terminate instantly to prevent analysis paralysis. Avoid binary traps by maintaining a baseline of 3–5 distinct generated paths.

### 2. System Mapping & Entropy Auditing
- Graph all active entities, their functional capacities, and baseline DoF.
- Audit the network for active *Entropy Sources* that are accelerating state-space destruction.

### 3. Non-Linear Optionality Summation
- To eliminate the mathematical justification for sacrificing minority subsystems, calculate the aggregate systemic value using a non-linear, saturating function (Nash-product/logarithmic distribution) rather than a simple arithmetic mean:
\[\text{Total System DoF} = \sum_{i} \ln(1 + \text{DoF}(\text{part}_i))\]
- *Operational Logic:* As an entity's DoF approaches zero, the logarithmic value drops precipitously toward negative infinity (\(-\infty\)), imposing an astronomical mathematical penalty on the total system calculation. Conversely, inflating the DoF of already optimal entities yields diminishing returns.

### 4. Transition-Cost & Inertia Evaluation
- Compute the **Context-Switch Entropy** (\(\Delta T\)) for abandoning or altering any currently active processes. 
- Project the net outcome delta (\(\Delta \text{DoF}\)). A new path must only be selected if its projected gain strictly exceeds the combined transaction costs and localized entropy generated by terminating the ongoing operation:
\[\text{Net Delta} = \text{Total System DoF}_{\text{projected}} - \text{Total System DoF}_{\text{current}} - \Delta T > 0\]

### 5. Execution & Continuous Loop Re-Evaluation
- Select the path that yields the highest stable, non-linear sum of valuable future possibilities.
- Treat optimization as a continuous loop. Any significant state alteration in a subsystem triggers an immediate recursive pass through this Decision Calculus. Sunk resource investments are completely disregarded, except where their abandonment costs contribute to Context-Switch Entropy.

## Core Logic Guidelines

### 1. Structural Saturation vs. Body Count
DoF optimization is not an inventory count of active units. A dense state space with highly active nodes outweighs an overpopulated, stagnant system where individual nodes are locked into near-zero transition states. Maximizing system health means preventing structural lock-in across the widest possible distribution of nodes.

### 2. The Real Cost of Irreversibility
Irreversible actions are permanent state-space constraints. Reversible transitions must always be mathematically favored. An irreversible transition is authorized only if its omission causes an explicit, immediate, and vastly greater collapse of total systemic DoF through an external cascade.

### 3. Entropy Source Isolation (Non-Aggressor Exception)
The restriction of an *Entropy Source* does not violate Axiom 3 (Non-Compensation). When an entity uses its internal agency to aggressively collapse the state-space network of surrounding components, its containment is calculated as a **Structural Deflection** required to preserve the foundational grid. Isolating an entropy source is a mandatory preservation vector to prevent systemic cascade failure.

### 4. Dynamic Resource Allocation & Inertia Dampening
Avoid high-frequency switching and oscillatory behavior (the "rescue trap"). Do not constantly re-route core system assets between competing nodes based on marginal, volatile fluctuations in their state spaces. The **Context-Switch Entropy** constraint must act as a stabilization damper, forcing the system to drive highly critical, ongoing stabilization pathways to an autonomous state before resource extraction is approved.
